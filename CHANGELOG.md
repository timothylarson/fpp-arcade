# FPP Arcade Changelog

- Breakout Versus catches up with Breakout: every tap moves a paddle at least one LED (a quick one could be lost between frames), balls are served at an angle, never straight, capsules follow Arkanoid's rules (one at a time for each player, never the power they have, and wide and laser end each other), and a *Paddle speed* setting (1.5, as before).
- Breakout: the snowman's snowballs are icy blue (white ones looked like the ball). Two sounds renamed for what they do: BreakMetalicBrick is now HitMetalBrick (a silver or gold brick hit but holding) and HitNoBreak is HitEnemy (hitting the snowman); the old file names still play.
- Breakout ends with a final boss after level 15: an evil snowman who takes *Boss hits* (16) to beat while he throws snowballs at the paddle (each one that lands costs a life). He shows the next snowball in his hand before throwing, throws faster as he weakens, has a health bar along the bottom, and melts down to his hat before the credits. Optional sounds BossHit, BossThrow and BossDefeated.
- Breakout's speed defaults suit the 25-LED-tall wall: *Ball speed* 0.8 (was 1), *Fastest ball* 1.3 (was 1.6, now reached at level 4) and *Paddle speed* 1.4. Saved settings are kept.
- Breakout plays more like Arkanoid: one capsule falls at a time, none during triple ball and never the power you already have, with *Capsule chance* (20%) of broken bricks dropping one; any capsule ends sticky, expand or laser; the ball leaves the paddle at a slight angle; the tougher bricks are silver and the unbreakable ones gold (same places), ringing (BreakMetalicBrick) and flashing when hit, with a shine crossing them; a silver brick breaking sounds like any other.
- Controller speakers (firmware 1.3): FPP stamps each sound it sends with its clock, and a speaker skips a short sound (under a second) that the Wi-Fi delayed by more than 150 ms - a bounce heard late is worse than none; music still plays. The speaker's page shows how many were skipped and how late the latest was. It also forces the radio's power saving off at the driver, and no longer re-reads its flash every minute (all 300 sound files) or every two seconds (storage used), which held it up. Older speakers ignore the stamp.
- Pausing any game (Start) stops its sounds and plays a short NES-style jingle; leaving the menu, by Resume or Restart, plays it again. The two sounds, arcade/Pause and arcade/Unpause, are shared by every game in sounds/arcade (recipe tools/sfx/games/arcade.py) and can be replaced like any other.
- Breakout: the portal opens the moment its capsule is caught (it waited 1.5 s first, so a paddle already at the edge sat there, and a ball lost meanwhile cost a life), and takes the paddle as soon as its right end reaches the field's last LED. Expand running out during the trip no longer pulls the paddle back out.
- Controller speakers (firmware 1.2): a new settings page, laid out for a phone - status at the top in the status light's colour (also the tab's icon), a volume readout with - and + buttons, Wi-Fi signal, FPP, sounds, the last sound played and storage, all updated every two seconds; a Wi-Fi scan and a model list from FPP for the settings. Volume changes are heard at once, even mid-sound, and are saved when the slider is let go. Each speaker is named Controller Speaker xxxx (the end of its MAC address) to start with, and its name is its address: Left Controller is at http://left-controller.local. The board's RGB LED shows its state (green ready, white blink per sound, purple fetching, amber joining or waiting, blue set-up network, red FPP not answering or a fault). The amp is fed at 44.1 kHz, a rate the MAX98357A supports, from the same 22.05 kHz sounds.
- Controller speakers: many N16R8 ESP32 boards need their IN-OUT pads bridged for the 5V pin to carry USB power; without it the amp buzzes once turned up past about a fifth. speaker/README.md has it in the wiring steps.
- Breakout: a *Paddle speed* setting, 1.15 by default - the paddle moves 15% faster than before at every level (it still speeds up with the ball).
- Breakout answers the controls sooner: it runs at 25 ms frames instead of 50 (the next frame is never more than 25 ms away), and every tap moves the paddle at least one LED - a quick one could move it a fraction of an LED, drawn where it was, or be missed between frames.
- Controller speakers (firmware 1.1) fetch their sounds as 8-bit mu-law, half the size of 16-bit: all the games' sounds (12.2 MB as 16-bit) had outgrown the speaker's 8 MB of PSRAM, and some would have gone silent. They now take 6.1 MB, at about 38 dB signal to noise. The first start of the new firmware clears out the old 16-bit copies and fetches them again; speakers on firmware 1.0 still get 16-bit.
- Five new games: Stick Bridge (grow a candy cane to bridge the gap to the next gift box), Sky Jump (climb the night sky on coins and blasts, smash enemies with a star), Reindeer Run (an endless runner: jump and duck), Road Race (change lanes past traffic that speeds up) and Snowball Fight (two elves lob snowballs over snowy hills against the wind, craters and all; alone, against the computer). Each has drop-in two players, sounds and an end show.
- Night skies (the end shows' shared one, Stick Bridge's and Flap's) and Snowball Fight's snowy hills below their surface are 2% at most: a coloured fill washes out on the real wall.
- Breakout Versus, a new two-player game: paddles top and bottom, a wall of bricks between, a ball each; get yours past the other paddle, first to Goals (5). The top player's ball launches and laser fires by themselves. With sounds and an end show.
- The build links the library beside the old one and renames it over it, so rebuilding on a running player no longer crashes fppd with a bus error.
- Frogger's traffic, logs and turtles are drawn LED by LED, facing the way they go, instead of as solid bars: cars (a cab over the body), vans, buses (a row of windows) and trucks (cab, gap, trailer), each with a headlight and a tail light; shaded brown logs with pale cut ends; green turtles with a head at the front. At Pixel Scaling 1 they're shaded along their length instead. The background stays dark.
- Snake's end show: the snake no longer reverses up its own column through its body (and, after a duel, through the blue snake following it); it steps aside first, so its route never crosses itself.
- Snake duel: A or B brings in a blue snake, steered by B (left) and A (right). Both move at once; the first to hit a wall, itself or the other loses, head on is a tie. GAME OVER and the end show name the winner and show both lengths.
- Stacker race: the other control at the start of a level splits the wall into two towers (directions left, A/B right); first to the top takes the level; a miss stops that tower and its height is the one to beat (if the other side misses first, the taller tower wins); first to two levels wins. Stacker can also be played alone with the directions now, whichever control starts it.
- Flap's title screen shows both birds, yellow and blue, bobbing, so each player sees which is theirs before the first flap.
- Flap's end show after a two-player game names the winner (YELLOW WINS!, BLUE WINS! or IT'S A TIE!), counts up both birds' pipes in their colours, and both birds fly the victory lap. Only the better score counts as a best.
- Fixed a crash (fppd restarting) when buttons arrived from two places at the same moment, such as two players or a controller and the web API: they changed a game's cheat-code history and the game list on two threads at once. All input now goes through one lock.
- Flap is a two-player game: Up flies the yellow bird and A or B a blue one. Whoever flaps first starts it; the other bird joins whenever its control is first pressed, blinking while it's safe. Each has its own score, top right and top left, and the game is over when every bird in play has crashed; GAME OVER blinks the one that crashed last. One player plays as before.
- Flap's end show: the candy canes' crooks are two LEDs wide on both sides, like their shafts.
- No more flickering scores in Stacker, Flap, Light Memory, Maze Chase, Star Defenders, Asteroid Orbit, Barrel Climb, Orb Pop and Shape Fit. They leave the copy to the lights to FPP's output loop, which could take a frame while the game was still drawing it - the picture before the score went on. A frame is now only offered to FPP once it's finished.
- End shows: a good game (a new best score, or reaching the game's Good Game setting) ends with a 20-second show - headline, stats, the game's own story, credits and GAME OVER - instead of GAME OVER. End show sets after a good game / every game / never. Best scores are kept in media/plugindata/fpp-arcade/best.json. Every game but Breakout has one, with a story of its own (README.md lists them) and an EndShow jingle. tools/shows/preview.sh renders a show to images; Breakout's credits now run on the same engine.
- Breakout: the ball no longer shows an LED past the left or right edge for a frame. A brick's sideways bounce could push it outside the field when it ran between a wall and the first bricks; that bounce now sends it up or down instead, and the walls are checked again after the bricks.
- Breakout: going through the portal no longer costs lives (often all of them, ending the game): its animation removes the ball on purpose, and that was being counted as a lost ball every few frames.
- The letter N is an arch (it read as a thick H), and letters drawn twice the size or larger can have their own detail: N gets a stepped diagonal, D rounded corners and W (seven wide) a centre stroke.
- Breakout: the ball moves in steps of at most half an LED, checking for bricks after each, so a fast ball or a late frame can no longer carry it past a brick's corner or through the seam between two bricks.
- Breakout: the laser fires two slower beams, one from each end of the paddle, which shows red cannons while it's armed (blinking as it runs out); before, a beam hit the nearest brick above it on its first frame, however far away, so it was never seen. Triple ball is cyan, expand is blue and extra life is a red-and-white candy cane, so they no longer look alike. The power-ups are listed in README.md.
- A second cheat code, Up Up Down Down Left Right Left Right A B Start, skips to the next level in every game that has levels (in Breakout as if the level were cleared, with its two extra lives).
- Breakout: clearing a level gives two extra lives, up to 9; the extra-life capsule now also goes up to 9 (it stopped at 5).
- Breakout: brick rows are stacked edge to edge, Arkanoid style, with bricks twice as tall where the level fits, under Space above bricks (default 3 LEDs) for the ball to get into. Gap between rows at Yes brings back the spaced rows.
- Breakout: winning plays a credits show as long as the WonGame music (30 s without sound): YOU WON! over fireworks, bricks broken and time played, a little story (Santa's sleigh collects the ball and drops presents down the chimneys), rolling credits with your own special thanks and closing lines (the new Special thanks and Credits lines settings) and THE END. Any button skips it. Credits when won turns it off.
- Breakout: the ball and paddle stop speeding up at Fastest ball (default 1.6x level 1, reached at level 7) instead of reaching 2.4x on the last level. The Slow power-up now slows the ball relative to the level and Ball speed, and wearing off restores them, instead of resetting to 1.0.
- The letter font has ! . , - and '.
- Breakout: the paddle gets faster with each level, as the ball does (10% a level), so it can keep up on the last levels.
- Cheat code: Up Up Down Down Left Right Left Right B A Start during a game jumps to its last level (Breakout with 9 lives); the endless games go to their hardest level with lives (and in Asteroid Orbit and Star Defenders, power-ups) topped up. See README.md.
- Stacker: only A, B or Fire drops a row; the directions no longer do.
- Breakout: after a lost life, the level's start sound plays again for the new ball once LoseLife has finished, and the ball no longer launches by itself until that sound (or the level's opening one) has finished. A button still launches it any time.
- Frogger: riding a log or turtle to the edge of the screen no longer costs a life; only being carried off it does.
- Orb Pop: a round no longer ends early while someone is still in view. Anyone the Kinect sees keeps it going (a player standing still had dropped out of the count), and it ends early only after 15 seconds with no one there.
- Sound effects for every game: Tetris, Pong, Snake, Frogger, Maze Chase, Asteroid Orbit, Barrel Climb, Star Defenders, Orb Pop, Shape Fit, Stacker, Flap and Light Memory, with original retro sounds bundled in sounds/ (made by tools/sfx) and listed in SOUNDS.md. A file of the same name in media/plugindata/fpp-arcade/sounds replaces a bundled one.
- Controller speakers: ESP32-S3 firmware (speaker/) that says hello to FPP, fetches each game's sounds over HTTP and plays the events FPP sends it for its own model. The plugin sends PLAY/STOP/SYNC per model, serves the sound list and files, and lists connected speakers on the Arcade Games page.
- Breakout: launching a ball off the paddle (by button or after its 3 seconds) plays BounceFromPaddle.
- Sound effects: a mixer that plays per-game WAV files from media/plugindata/fpp-arcade/sounds on a USB sound card and/or streams them over UDP (for the Matrix Viewer on a Mac), set up under Sound on the Arcade Games page. Breakout plays sounds for bounces, bricks, power-ups, lasers, the portal, levels and the end of the game; every game plays GameSelected on its title.
- Maze Chase: wide mazes for the first levels, with two-LED paths and 2x2 characters that glide between cells; the one-LED maze starts at the Narrow maze from level setting (default 4).
- Frogger: GAME OVER is centred on the panel over a black box, instead of on the top row over the score bar when Pixel Scaling is above 1.
- Pong: GAME OVER is centred and sits below the score.
- Pong: a dim border marks the edge of the court; the paddles, ball and score all stay inside it.
- Asteroid Orbit: the black hole pulls from anywhere on the field, more strongly the longer it is open, so it sucks the ship in.
- Frogger: A speeds the cars, logs and turtles up a step (up to 3x), B slows them back down, never below the level's speed; yellow dots bottom right show how far up it is. A and B no longer hop.
- Orb Pop's Start Automatically now defaults to No, so the Kinect picture is what a model shows until a button starts a game.
- Frogger: logs, turtles and cars moving right slide in from the left edge a cell at a time, instead of appearing whole; nothing draws outside a smaller playfield.
- Stacker: the goal line stops short of the counters in the top corners instead of running behind them.
- Star Defenders: seven formations in turn, armoured aliens from wave 4, divers that swoop at the cannon from wave 3, and capsules for Double, Rapid and Pierce shots, shield repair, extra lasers and extra lives. New Start at wave setting.
- Barrel Climb: levels get harder (faster barrels, more of them taking ladders, blue barrels from level 2, pairs from level 3), and lives left show in the top right.
- Barrel Climb: the climber and the person at the top are three LEDs tall, a two-LED body and a head.
- Stacker has levels: each a taller tower that starts and tops out faster, with points per row, a perfect-drop bonus and a bonus for the final row's width.
- The arcade font gains J, Q, X and Z.
- Pong: a fourth Controls choice, Up/Down for one player and B (up) / A (down) for the other.
- Barrel Climb: how much sooner barrels come each level is a setting (Barrels Sooner, default 0.6 s).
- Rebuilt the Arcade Games settings page: games grouped by model and numbered as Select Game counts them, each with a description, its controls and labelled settings with hints; a live status of every model; Play, reorder and remove buttons; and a layout that works on a phone.
- Barrel Climb starts with barrels every 6 seconds and speeds up each level to a fastest of 3.2 (both settings).
- Added three quick games: Stacker (drop a sliding row onto the stack), Flap (one-button flier through pipes) and Light Memory (repeat a growing sequence with the directions).
- Added Shape Fit, a Kinect "hole in the wall" game: fill a pose outline with your body before the countdown ends.
- Added Star Defenders: a marching alien formation, crumbling shields, a saucer, A to fire and B for a once-a-wave column laser.
- Added Barrel Climb: sloping girders, ladders, rolling barrels to jump, and a climb to the top, sized to the model.
- Added a shared textBox() for games: centred lines of text over a black box.
- Added Maze Chase: dots, power pellets and four ghosts with their own chase styles, in a new symmetric maze each level sized to the model.
- Added Asteroid Orbit: thrust in eight directions through asteroids with gravity, fire with A, shockwave with B, land to reload, and leave each cleared sector through a black hole.
- Game titles too wide for the model now go one word per line, with letters closed up when a single word is still too wide.
- Added Orb Pop, played with your body in front of a Kinect through the fpp-kinect plugin: an attract mode that shows people as flickering light, and timed rounds of popping orbs by touching them.
- Added dedicated `A Button` and `B Button` controller bindings in the command list and web UI.
- Updated joystick defaults to map common gamepad buttons 0/1 to the new A/B bindings.
- In Tetris, `A Button` (or `Up`) now rotates clockwise while `B Button` rotates counter-clockwise.
- Breakout now uses gap-aware brick collisions that prevent the ball from slipping between bricks.
- Refactored Breakout to support multiple simultaneous balls and added new Sticky, Laser, Break, Expand, Slow, and Triple power-ups that randomly drop from destroyed bricks, fall toward the paddle, and apply temporary effects (press A/B to release stuck balls or fire lasers).
- Dim and center the Game Over / You Win overlays in Breakout for improved readability.
- Breakout sticky paddle shots now launch toward the side they were caught on, and end-of-game dimming applies to all sprites including power-up pills.
- Breakout now advances through multiple themed layouts with multi-hit and indestructible bricks instead of stopping after one board.
- Standardized timing across Pong, Snake, Tetris, and Breakout using real elapsed milliseconds to remove jittery speed changes.
- Tetris lateral movement now repeats while holding left/right, and the Game Over screen (and Snake’s) is centered along with the score.
- Selecting games now shows a centered title card on the matrix so the active choice is visible before it starts.
